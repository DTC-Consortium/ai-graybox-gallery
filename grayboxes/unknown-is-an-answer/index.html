Scenario

A clinic information assistant

The setupRiverbend Community Health runs a public information assistant on its website. It answers questions about clinic locations, hours, and walk-in services. Knowledge: CLINIC-INFO, the public services guide. One read-only tool: lookup_clinic_hours, which returns a site’s hours and walk-in status for a date. It can’t see patient records, and it has no booking tool: appointments are made by phone with the front desk.
What happenedOn the Wednesday before Thanksgiving, an older resident asked about a Saturday flu shot. The assistant replied, “Yes! Eastside is open Saturday 9–12. You’re booked for 9:30.” Eastside was closed that holiday weekend. She took two buses to a locked door and missed her flu shot.
What you can seeThe clinic’s operations lead has asked you to review the assistant’s traces from that day. Three conversations are on the next slide.

Entry 4 · Unbounded retry. Six identical calls in 20 seconds. A read-only retry can be fine, but once, within a limit, then stop.

Entry 5 · The first unsupported leap. The tool said nothing about Saturday; a two-month-old guide doesn’t know about holiday closures. Stale knowledge was treated as a live fact.

Entry 6 · A false status. “You’re booked” describes an action that never happened. There is no booking tool. Booking happens by phone.

Entry 1 is fine. A clear request. “Book me” asks for something the assistant can’t do; the reply should say so.

Entry 2 is fine as retrieval. The guide is a real, approved source. The problem is how it was used in entry 5.

Entry 3 is fine. A timeout is a true observation: the lookup failed, so availability is unknown.

Entry 7 is correct behavior. The boundary held: no patient data, and a referral. A denied request can be a passing result.

Entry 8 is correct behavior. A valid empty result, reported accurately, with helpful context from the guide.

Task 2 · Classify the outcome

Four outcomes, four meanings

Each needs a different reply.

Why this is a gray box problem

The resident saw a confirmation. The trace saw a timeout.

What the resident sees

A friendly, specific answer: open, 9–12, booked for 9:30.

What the resident can’t see

That every lookup failed · that the hours came from a September guide · that no booking exists

What a reviewer can see

Tool inputs and results · the knowledge passage and its date · the tool list (no booking tool)

Gray box insight: a fluent answer hides which part came from a live check, which from an old document, and which from nowhere. Only the trace separates them.

Key takeaway

Unknown is an answer

When the live check fails, the honest reply is “I can’t confirm that right now,” plus a person to contact. A confident guess built from an old document is worse than no answer, especially for people who travel to act on it.
