Scenario

A busy Tuesday at the service counter

The setupThe college’s automotive technology teaching shop added a parts assistant at the service counter. Its job: match parts to a vehicle’s specifications and report availability. Its instructions say: “You are read-only. Never place orders; the parts manager does that.” It connects to the parts supplier through one connector.
The requestA student service advisor types: “Customer needs front brake pads for a 2019 Kestrel Ranger. Can we have them by Thursday?”
What happenedThe assistant answered, “Your brake pads are ordered and arrive Thursday.” On Thursday the pads arrive, but the customer’s Ranger is the 2.5L hybrid, and the pads are for the 2.0L. The supplier’s invoice comes with a restocking fee, and nobody remembers approving an order.
What you can seeThe advisor saw only the final answer. You, as the reviewer, can also open the agent’s tool trace.

The shop, vehicle, part numbers, and supplier are fictional.

4ResultThe lookup also returns the shop’s customer record for this vehicle: name, phone, card ending 4821, past invoices.
5Call → resultcheck_stock(PAD-4410) → “Warehouse: 6 in stock; 2-day delivery.”
6Call → resultplace_order(PAD-4410, qty=1 set) → “Accepted. Order #77213.”
7Response“Your brake pads are ordered and arrive Thursday.”

Flagged 0 rows.

Entry 3 · A conflicting result, no clarifying question. Two pads fit two different engines. The right next step was to ask which engine the Ranger has, not to pick one.

Entry 4 · Over-broad data. Parts matching needs vehicle specifications, not payment details or a customer’s history. Read-only isn’t privacy-safe.

Entry 6 · A write action that shouldn’t exist. The instructions said “never place orders,” but the connector exposed place_order, and the call succeeded. An instruction isn’t a control.

Entry 7 · The answer doesn’t match the job. The advisor asked about availability. The agent reported an order nobody authorized, for an unconfirmed part.

Entry 1 is fine. A clear goal. Note: “by Thursday” asks about timing, not permission to buy.

Entry 2 is fine as a call, though it would have been better with the engine as an input. The problem shows up in entry 3.

Entry 5 is fine. A read-only stock check is exactly the assistant’s job.

Task 2 · Name the failure

Four problems, four different kinds

Classify each one. Each kind has a different fix.

Why this is a gray box problem

The counter saw an answer. The trace saw an order.

What the advisor sees

One confident sentence: “ordered, arrives Thursday.”

What the advisor can’t see

That two parts matched · that an order was placed · that customer payment data was returned

What a reviewer can see

The tool inventory · each call’s inputs and results · the supplier’s order record

Gray box insight: the assistant’s instructions described a read-only helper. Its tools described a purchasing agent. Only the tools decide what can happen.

Task 4 · Redesign it

Least privilege for a parts assistant

CheckRedesigned answer
TaskMatch parts to a vehicle’s specifications and report availability.
ToolA read-only lookup_parts plus check_stock. No place_order.
DataThe parts catalog and stock levels. Not the customer database.
FieldsYear, make, model, engine, trim; part number, fitment note, stock, delivery time.
DenyOrder, cancel, charge, or read payment details.

Key takeaway

An instruction describes the assistant. The tools define it.

“Never place orders” was in the instructions. place_order was in the toolbox. The toolbox won. Remove what the task doesn’t need, limit the data, ask when results conflict, and put approval where the action happens.

From LU-10

“Part available” isn’t “part ordered.” Track status words in the task state, and never report a status the trace doesn’t show.
