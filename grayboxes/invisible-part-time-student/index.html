Scenario

A real question, a real problem

The situation · what you seeA part-time evening student asks the Department Help Assistant (DHA): “When is the last day to drop a course?” The DHA returns the full-time day-program deadline: technically in the handbook, accurate for one population, wrong for this student.
Why it happened · what you have to investigateThe handbook has both deadlines, but the evening-program section uses different heading styles. Retrieval ranks the day-program section higher because it contains more text and is referenced more often.
Who is affected · who bears the costPart-time, evening, and accelerated-program students: populations already underserved by institutional systems, and disproportionately working adults, caregivers, and first-generation students.

Why this is a gray box problem

Partial visibility demands an audit

What the student can see

The question they asked · the answer they received · possibly a source citation

What the student can’t see

The retrieval ranking · that a different section exists · the system prompt · the knowledge base structure

What even the builder may not see

Whether retrieval consistently favors the longer section · whether the evening section is ever retrieved for any query

Gray box insight: you know enough to test, but not enough to predict. Partial visibility is what makes an audit essential.

Key takeaway

Accurate on average, wrong for a subgroup

A system can be accurate on average and systematically wrong for a subgroup. Bias testing requires testing with the subgroup’s context, not just the system’s most common use case.
